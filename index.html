Recruiting, Academics & Beyond

Basketball is a tool to help you reach your academic and professional goals. Explore the resources below to register with the NCAA, secure academic funding, evaluate trade schools, and connect directly with college basketball programs.

NCAA Eligibility Center Registration

Any student-athlete who wants to compete at an NCAA Division I or II school must create an Academic and Athletics Certification Account to receive their official NCAA ID. Division III athletes or undecided underclassmen should start by creating a free Profile Page.

  • Ensure you use the exact legal name that appears on your high school transcripts.
  • Ask your high school guidance counselor to upload your official transcripts to the Eligibility Center at the end of each academic year.

KEES & Academic Scholarships

Athletic scholarships are highly competitive, making academic excellence the most reliable pathway to funding your college education. Local programs heavily reward strong academic performance.

Kentucky Educational Excellence Scholarship (KEES)

The Kentucky Educational Excellence Scholarship (KEES) is a program that allows students to earn money for college by achieving good grades in high school and qualifying scores on the ACT or SAT. To be eligible for a KEES yearly GPA award, high school students must earn at least a 2.5 GPA in any year of high school while meeting the KEES curriculum requirements. The better students perform academically, the more they can earn toward their college education. A student's KEES award expires when eight semesters of KEES funding has been used, five years have passed since high school graduation, or upon earning a bachelor's degree.

FAFSA & Additional Aid

Regardless of your division level, all prospective student-athletes should fill out the Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA) during their senior year to qualify for Pell Grants and institution-specific academic grants.

Trade Schools & Vocational Pathways

A traditional four-year university isn't the only path to a highly successful career. Trade schools, technical colleges, and vocational apprenticeships offer accelerated, specialized training for high-demand, high-paying industries (e.g., HVAC, electrical, welding, aviation mechanics, IT).

  • Shorter Duration: Programs typically take 6 months to 2 years to complete.
  • Lower Debt: Significantly reduced tuition costs compared to traditional 4-year institutions.
  • Athletic Continuation: Many community and technical colleges feature NJCAA (Junior College) basketball programs, allowing you to play highly competitive basketball while earning your certification or preparing to transfer to a four-year university.
